## Key Synthesis

The workflow should not ask "What is profitable?" first. Ask:

```text
What do you already have?
What has evidence?
Who has a related painful situation?
What small test can reveal whether this matters?
```

## From Self To Venture

Use this bridge:

```text
Experience -> Capability -> Transferable Pattern -> Audience Pain -> Offer Hypothesis -> Validation Test
```

Example:

```text
I have done data migration projects
-> I can design quality checks under messy constraints
-> I notice failure points in system transitions
-> small companies changing tools fear data mismatch
-> "system migration risk checklist" or consulting diagnostic
-> test with 5 operators/project managers and 3 posts about migration mistakes
```

## Evidence Ladder

Treat evidence as more important than confidence.

1. **Claim**: "I think I am good at this."
2. **Personal proof**: completed work, certificates, deliverables, repeated practice.
3. **External proof**: others asked for help, paid, reused, recommended, or relied on it.
4. **Market proof**: strangers show attention, commitment, payment, or repeated demand.

Do not move to product recommendations when the evidence is only claim-level unless the output is clearly labeled as a hypothesis.

## Common Misreadings To Correct

- "I have done many things" is not yet an asset map. Extract problems solved and proof.
- "I like it" is not market demand. Translate it to a user task and validate.
- "It is hot now" is not a fit. Check self-fit, access, and affordable loss.
- "I have no result" does not mean no asset. It may mean the offer must be earlier-stage, such as a worksheet, diagnostic, or experiment log.
- "I must find the one right direction" is a design trap. Generate multiple prototypes.
