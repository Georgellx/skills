# Assets and rights contract

## Establish provenance

Maintain an asset manifest for every external visual, font, sound effect, music track, AI-generated asset, and downloaded reference. Record:

- local filename and timeline use;
- original page and direct asset URL when available;
- creator or provider;
- license name, version, and access date;
- commercial-use and attribution requirements;
- modifications made;
- user-supplied rights declaration or generation job ID.

Keep downloaded files under `edit/downloads/` and the manifest under `edit/assets-manifest.json`.

## Select usable material

Use current web research when sourcing is required. Prefer first-party libraries, official public-domain archives, and providers with explicit licenses. Open the asset page and verify the license rather than relying on search-result summaries.

Select high-resolution, watermark-free assets that remain legible after 9:16 cropping. Mark archival, stock, illustrative, or AI-generated imagery honestly in the internal plan and avoid presenting it as the speaker's personal history.

## Preserve reference boundaries

Extract design grammar from a reference while creating project-owned content. Carry over proportions, hierarchy, rhythm, and motion principles only to the extent approved. Exclude the reference creator's name, logo, watermark, distinctive copy, footage, face, and audio unless documented reuse rights exist.

## Control music and paid generation

Follow the workspace music default. Adding music, cloning a voice, generating a person, or starting a paid asset job requires explicit authorization for that task. State provider, model, call count, maximum cost, and rights implications before the first paid request.

Persist provider IDs and request fingerprints immediately. Reuse completed jobs and downloaded originals rather than submitting blind retries.
