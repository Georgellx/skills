# Intake and approval contract

## Establish context

Inspect the supplied footage, reference media, images, transcripts, existing `edit/` artifacts, and workspace rules before asking questions. Summarize what the material actually supports.

Resolve these dimensions when they are not already explicit:

- target platform, aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, and delivery filename;
- target duration or acceptable range;
- content that must remain, may be tightened, or may be removed;
- reference priority when more than one reference is supplied;
- visual identity, brand assets, subtitles, music, and color treatment;
- external asset rights, AI-generation permission, paid-service permission, and cost ceiling;
- prototype requirement and approval owner.

Ask one to three focused questions at a time. Recommend the option best supported by the material and state its tradeoff. Continue useful read-only analysis while a non-blocking answer is pending.

## Interpret approval precisely

Record each approval in `edit/project.md` with:

- artifact name and path;
- approved version or checksum when material;
- approval time or session;
- scope of approval;
- later feedback that invalidates it.

Use these gates:

1. **Strategy approval:** authorizes EDL and design planning.
2. **Style approval:** authorizes motion implementation for a new or changed direction.
3. **Prototype approval:** authorizes extension of that style across the full timeline.
4. **Preview approval:** authorizes the final-quality render.

An approved standing template may satisfy the style and prototype gates when the current task does not change its layout, typography, motion, or portrait treatment. Record why the gate was reused.

## Trigger a fresh confirmation

Return to the affected gate when any of these changes:

- narrative meaning, audience, CTA, or required duration;
- reference precedence or visual identity;
- portrait framing that removes a meaningful face, gesture, hand action, or object;
- subtitles changing from verbatim to rewritten text;
- music, voice, AI imagery, licensed media, or paid-provider usage;
- delivery platform, aspect ratio, resolution, or frame rate.

## Write the strategy in plain language

Cover the following in a compact proposal:

- opening hook and narrative arc;
- edit density and treatment of slips, repetition, pauses, and emotional air;
- full-screen portrait versus information-scene portrait windows;
- key visual beats and reference-derived motion language;
- caption chunking, placement, and emphasis;
- audio and music policy;
- estimated runtime, prototype scope, preview, and final output.

Keep tool and implementation details out of the approval summary unless they create a visible tradeoff.
